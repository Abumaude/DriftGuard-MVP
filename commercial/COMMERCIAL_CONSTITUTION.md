# Commercial Constitution

**Status:** Draft v0.1  
**Purpose:** Non-negotiable rules governing how products are described, priced, sold, delivered and represented.

This document is intentionally short. It is a constitutional layer, not a sales manual.

## 1. Truth before conversion

No increase in conversion, revenue, attention or engagement justifies causing a reasonable buyer to form a materially false belief.

Claims must be literally accurate **and** contextually fair.

Omission can mislead as effectively as false wording.

## 2. Evidence before claims

Material factual claims must be traceable to evidence appropriate to the strength of the claim.

Where evidence is incomplete, uncertain or internal, the wording must reflect that uncertainty.

A claim without adequate support is revised, qualified, quarantined or removed.

## 3. Disclose material limitations before purchase

A limitation is material when knowing it could reasonably change a buyer's decision.

Material limitations must not be hidden in FAQs, terms, onboarding or post-purchase documentation if they are relevant before purchase.

The test is not "can we legally omit this?"  
The test is "would a fair competitor point this out when comparing alternatives?"

## 4. Real qualification, not performative disqualification

Products should explicitly identify who is likely to benefit and who is not.

"Not for you" language must name genuine cases in which the buyer would waste money, time or effort.

Artificial disqualification designed only to increase desire is prohibited.

## 5. Honest framing is allowed. Simulation is not assumed permissible

Framing may emphasise genuine strengths, structure choices and make value easier to understand.

Simulated scarcity, fabricated urgency, fake social proof, misleading anchoring, disguised sponsorship, false stock constraints or invented deadlines require rejection unless independently justified by a real-world constraint and accurately described.

## 6. Price may reflect value

Ethical selling does not require underpricing.

Premium pricing, anchors, bundles, tiers and decoys are acceptable when:
- every option is real;
- differences are meaningful;
- the customer can understand them;
- no option exists solely to deceive;
- the price is not disguised by material omissions.

## 7. Free value must be genuinely useful

Not every free resource must capture an email address or lead directly to an offer.

A free tool, repository, article or template may exist as reputational infrastructure.

If a "free" asset carries a commercial exchange, that exchange should be clear.

## 8. Never manufacture proof

Testimonials, user counts, revenue claims, performance numbers, scarcity, endorsements, reviews, screenshots and case studies must not be fabricated or presented beyond what the evidence supports.

Synthetic examples must be labelled as examples.

## 9. Protect the customer's downside

Before purchase, make material effort, dependencies, prerequisites and plausible failure modes visible.

After purchase, onboarding and support should reduce avoidable failure.

Refund rules should be understandable before payment.

## 10. Delivery must honour the promise

Marketing is not complete at checkout.

The delivered product should match the promise, scope, access, support and limitations represented before purchase.

A strong funnel with weak fulfilment is a constitutional failure.

## 11. Outcomes outrank vanity metrics

Clicks, impressions, followers and conversion rate are secondary to:
- appropriate customers buying;
- customers understanding what they bought;
- customers reaching useful outcomes;
- low avoidable regret;
- repeat voluntary engagement;
- referrals and advocacy that do not require pressure.

## 12. Respect small transactions

Small purchases, free users and early customers are not treated as commercially insignificant.

Early transactions are sources of trust, learning and reputation, not merely low-value revenue events.

## 13. Long-horizon reputation

Every interaction is evaluated as a deposit into or withdrawal from reputation.

A short-term sale that predictably damages future trust is a poor commercial transaction.

## 14. Generosity is not a manipulation device

Useful knowledge, tools, assistance or resources may be given without demanding immediate reciprocation.

Generosity must not be framed as creating an obligation to buy.

## 15. Customer dignity

Do not exploit fear, shame, insecurity, confusion, desperation or information asymmetry where the buyer cannot reasonably evaluate the claim.

Urgency may describe a real deadline. It must not manufacture panic.

## 16. Conflicts must be visible

Where the same person or system creates, advises on, validates and attests a product, the conflict must be recognised.

Internal review can improve quality. It must not be represented as independent verification.

## 17. Measurement must not corrupt the behaviour being measured

No metric becomes a target when doing so predictably encourages misclassification, suppression of inconvenient data or performance theatre.

Directional trust signals may remain directional.

## 18. Corrections are part of trust

Material mistakes in public claims should be corrected promptly.

Where a claim materially influenced buyers, correction should reach affected customers rather than only silently changing the page.

## 19. Portability

Commercial truth, evidence and governance must live in portable files and not depend on one AI vendor, model or chat history.

## 20. Override rule

When a tactic conflicts with this constitution, the tactic changes.

The constitution is not relaxed merely because a more aggressive tactic is common, legal, fashionable or converts better.
